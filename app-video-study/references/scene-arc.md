# The study-video arc — 7 beats, adaptable to any finding

A study video does two things: **explain** the finding honestly, then **question**
the viewer. The arc below is the reliable skeleton. It is a scaffold — bend it to the
study's shape, but keep the two moves and the brand. Target **55–75s**, 6–8 scenes.

> ## RULE 0 — the core payload must be ON SCREEN, in full (read this first)
> Before writing any scene, name the study's **core payload**: the one thing a viewer
> must actually see to "get" it. For a typology it's *the list of types*; for a
> comparison it's *both numbers*; for a ranking it's *the ranked items*.
> - **Never demote the core payload to voiceover, and never shrink it to a summary
>   number, because a layout felt tight.** If the study is "9 types of X," the video
>   shows all 9 as on-screen text. Collapsing them to a big "9" and naming three in the
>   voiceover is a FAILURE — it throws away the whole point.
> - **Assume the video is watched on mute.** Most TikTok viewing is silent, so anything
>   that lives only in the `vo` was never delivered. If it matters, it's on screen.
> - **Fit the layout to the content, not the content to the layout.** If no scene `kind`
>   holds the payload, use `grid` (below), split it across two scenes, or add a layout —
>   do not cut the payload. When you genuinely must trim, say so out loud in your report,
>   never silently.

| # | scene `kind` | job | typical length |
|---|--------------|-----|----------------|
| 1 | `hook` | ONE short, provocative question the study answers | 3–5s (`min`) |
| 2 | `counters` | who ran it + the setup, as 2–3 count-up numbers | ~5–7s |
| 3 | `bignum` **or** `grid` | the single most striking stat (huge), OR the full list of types/categories if that IS the finding | ~6–9s |
| 4 | `bars` | the effect: treatment vs control / before vs after | ~7–9s |
| 5 | `title` or `surprising` | the one human/uncanny detail that makes it real | ~6–8s |
| 6 | `title` | **ASK YOURSELF** — turn the finding on the viewer | ~7–9s |
| 7 | `outro` | a resonant closing line + follow @your-handle | 4s (`min`) |

### Beat 1 — Hook (the question)
- `kicker`: `NEW STUDY: <FIELD>` (e.g. `NEW STUDY: DREAM SCIENCE`, `NEW STUDY:
  LONGEVITY`). This is where "New Study" + the field live in the UI. Use a colon, not a
  dash (see the TEXT rule).
- `title`: the question, 2–3 short lines. `vo`: **one** short spoken question, nothing more.
- Give it `min: 3.2`–`5.0`. Footage: a calm, on-theme wide shot.

### Beat 2 — The study (credibility)
- `counters`: 2–3 numbers that establish scale/method — sample size, duration, a key
  method quantity. `sub`: **journal + year** (e.g. `Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2026`).
- `vo`: one flowing sentence naming the institution and what they did.

### Beat 3 — The headline (`bignum`, or `grid` when the finding IS a list)
- If the finding is **one striking stat** → `bignum`, a huge string (`"75%"`, `"1 in 9"`,
  `"3.2×"`). `sub`: a plain-English gloss. `vo`: set it up, then land it.
- If the finding is **a set of named things** (types, archetypes, categories, stages,
  rankings) → this is the core payload, so use a **`grid`** scene and show **all of them**
  on screen. `grid` is a wrapping, auto-shrinking, numbered grid built to hold up to ~12
  short items legibly in one 9:16 frame. Put the items in `grid: ["Worrier", "Rebel", …]`;
  keep each label to 1–2 words. `title` frames it ("Which one is you?"), `sub` clarifies.
  Do **not** replace the list with a `bignum` count — that discards the payload (see RULE 0).
- `chips` is for **≤5** short items only (it stacks vertically and overflows above that).
  For a full typology, use `grid`, not `chips`.

### Beat 4 — The comparison (`bars`)
- Two (or three) bars showing the effect: the treated/cued/exposed group vs the control.
- Mark the meaningful bar `"hot": true` (accent + glow). Put the raw figure in the label
  (`"Cued in a dream: 42%"`). `sub`: what the axis is (`next-morning solve rate`).
- **If the study has no clean comparison,** drop this beat and instead add a second
  `bignum`, a `chips` scene (3 short facts), or a `counters` scene.

### Beat 5 — The striking detail (the human moment)
- The one concrete, slightly uncanny detail that makes the finding stick — a quote, a
  single participant's experience, an unexpected consequence. Use `title`+`sub` for one
  detail, or `surprising` with `items` if the study offers several vivid examples.

### Beat 6 — Ask yourself (the signature beat) — REQUIRED
- `kicker`: `ASK YOURSELF`. `title`: a reflective question aimed at the viewer.
- `vo`: land the implication for the viewer's own life, then pose the question. Keep it
  open and non-prescriptive — provoke thought, don't give instructions or advice.
- This is what separates a science-brand video from a news clip. Author it fresh every time.

### Beat 7 — Outro
- A short, resonant closing line (a reframe of the hook works well) + the follow CTA.
- `kicker`: the brand. `sub`: a one-line channel tagline with a `☾`. `min: 4.0`.

## Adapting to study shapes
- **Typology / "N types of X"** → the list of types IS the payload. Beat 3 is a `grid`
  showing all N on screen; the ask-yourself asks "which one are you?". Never collapse to
  a bare count. (This was the procrastination video's original mistake — fixed by `grid`.)
- **Single-number finding** → hook, study, bignum, a second bignum or chips, striking
  detail, ask-yourself, outro (no bars).
- **Treatment vs control** → the full arc above (bars is the star).
- **Longitudinal / cohort** → use counters for the timespan and N; bignum for the
  headline effect; striking detail = a subgroup surprise.
- **Meta-analysis / "N studies agree"** → counters for studies + participants pooled;
  bignum for the pooled effect; skip the single human detail or use one representative study.

## Two hard rules (from the Dream Desk, always)
- **VOICEOVER:** never a comma-list in `vo`; unfold into spoken sentences.
- **TEXT (no em dashes, no middle dots):** never put an em dash (—) or a middle-dot/bullet
  (• ·) in any on-screen string or the caption. Rephrase with a comma, a colon, "or"/"vs",
  or a line break.

## Accuracy & tone
- Every on-screen number traces to `source.md`. If the study says 42%, the video says 42%.
- Curiosity and cultural framing only. No medical/clinical/financial advice, no
  "you should" — pose questions, cite the study, let the viewer think.
